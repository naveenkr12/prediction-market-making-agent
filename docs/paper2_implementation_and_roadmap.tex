\pagebanner{01 / IMPLEMENTED CORE}{Paper 2: From Trade Flow to Price Impact}
{\sffamily\color{muted} Implementation status, advancement beyond Paper 1, and remaining research}

\takeaway{\textbf{Contribution.} The project now connects Bartlett and O'Hara's empirical Kyle's $\lambda$ framework to a training-calibrated market-making environment and evaluates the existing HJB policy within it. This completes the core impact integration; it does not reproduce every analysis in Paper 2 or establish an improved optimal strategy.}

\section{Empirical foundation and estimator}
Paper 2 studies adverse selection in binary prediction markets: a maker's counterparties may trade on information that subsequently moves prices against the maker. Its main comparison is \emph{single-name} versus \emph{broad-based} markets \cite{bartlett}. Sports remains a separate class in our implementation.

The verified Kalshi sample contains \textbf{60 settled contracts, 68,866 trades and 14,318 active five-minute intervals}. Taker directions and fractional sizes are retained; block trades and duplicate IDs are excluded. Empty intervals are not interpolated. Every primary market fit has at least 50 usable active intervals. Event-preserving train/validation/test splits contain 36/12/12 contracts; overlapping histories mean this is not a strict chronological walk-forward split \cite{repo}.

For trade $i$, let $s_i=+1$ for a YES taker, $s_i=-1$ for a NO taker, and $c_i>0$ denote contracts. For market $j$ and five-minute bucket $h$:
\begin{equation}
 F_{j,h}=\sum_{i\in h}s_i c_i,\qquad
 \Delta L_{j,h}=\logit(p^{\rm last}_{j,h})-\logit(p^{\rm first}_{j,h}),
 \quad \logit(p)=\log\frac{p}{1-p}.
 \label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
Prices $p$ are YES probabilities. First and last prices are transactions \emph{within the same bucket}; endpoints at 0 or 1 are excluded from log-odds estimation. Paper 2's central regression (its Eq.~1) is implemented market by market:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta L_{j,h}=a_j+\lambda_j F_{j,h}+\epsilon_{j,h}.
 \label{eq:kyle}
\end{equation}
$\lambda_j$ is measured in \textbf{log odds per net contract}; a companion regression uses cents. HC1 and calendar-aware HAC standard errors, multiple-testing adjustments, endpoint/lifetime sensitivities and influential-bucket diagnostics extend the paper's point estimator. Negative estimates are retained. In this sample, 40/60 primary HAC intervals include zero, and 35/60 slopes change by more than 50\% when the highest-leverage bucket is removed.

\section{Translating measured lambda into the simulator}
Our project extension applies a signed-flow jump to latent log odds:
\begin{equation}
 L_{k+1}=L_k+\beta_g s_kc_k+\varepsilon_k,
 \qquad p_{k+1}=\frac{1}{1+e^{-L_{k+1}}},
 \label{eq:impact}
\end{equation}
where $g$ denotes market class and $\varepsilon_k$ is background movement. Flow is applied once, including the volume underlying any own fill. Since a post-trade opening reference already contains the first jump, $E_{j,h}=F_{j,h}-s_{\rm first}c_{\rm first}$. For zero-innovation reference replay, calibration uses
\begin{equation}
 r_j=\frac{\Cov(F_{j,h},E_{j,h})}{\Var(F_{j,h})},\qquad
 \lambda_j^{\rm replay}=\beta_g r_j,\qquad
 \beta_g=\frac{\max\{0,\operatorname{median}_{j\in g,\,\mathrm{train}}\widehat\lambda_j\}}
 {\operatorname{median}_{j\in g,\,\mathrm{train}}r_j}.
 \label{eq:mapping}
\end{equation}
Only \textbf{36 training contracts, 44,506 trades and 9,424 buckets} determine calibration. Base $\beta_g$ is $5.47379\times10^{-5}$ for broad-based and $1.26983\times10^{-5}$ for sports. Zero/low/base/high scenarios include estimation and sensitivity uncertainty; they are stress settings, not causal confidence bounds. Reference replay is verified; full execution-price lambda validation remains pending.

\section{What Paper 1 established}
Feil and Nendel model price as a conditional settlement probability and optimize bid/ask quotes using HJB control \cite{feil}. With cash $X$, inventory $q$, outcome $Y\in\{0,1\}$ and admissible quotes $\pi$, the original foundation is
\begin{align}
 dp_t&=\varsigma(t,p_t)dW_t,
 \qquad \varsigma(t,p)=p(1-p)\bar\sigma(t,p),
 \qquad Y\mid\mathcal F_T\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_T), \label{eq:paper1price}\\
 \sup_{\pi}\E\!\Bigl[X_T+q_TY
 &-\gamma\int_0^T q_t^2\varsigma(t,p_t)^2\,dt
 -\gamma_Tq_T^2p_T(1-p_T)\Bigr]. \label{eq:paper1objective}
\end{align}
The baseline solver, parameters and saved value function are preserved. Paper 1 includes price-, time- and quote-dependent execution intensities, inventory limits and settlement risk; it does not directly couple signed executions to reference-price jumps. Its theoretical optimum applies to its stated model.

\section{What the project now adds}
\textbf{Activity, sizes and noise.} Training templates retain ten lifetime phases, quiet periods and paired fractional size/side marks. A gamma--Poisson count model captures overdispersion. Background log-odds volatility is fitted from observed five-minute midpoint changes after removing their fitted linear flow association. Innovations resample standardized empirical residuals and scale as $\sigma_g\sqrt{\Delta t}$, with time in seconds. This residual component is not a causally identified fundamental-price process.

\textbf{Execution and accounting.} Post-only quotes connect to queue/participation scenarios, partial fills, cash reserves, collateral, fees and binary settlement. If $m_i$ is the signed maker quantity (buy positive), $P_i$ its actual execution price, and $f_i$ its fee:
\begin{equation}
 q_{i+1}=q_i+m_i,\quad C_{i+1}=C_i-P_im_i-f_i,\quad
 W_t=C_t+q_tp_t,\quad W_T=C_{T^-}+q_{T^-}Y.
 \label{eq:accounts}
\end{equation}
Fills are booked at standing quotes \emph{before} the aggregate-flow jump. Maker quantity is opposite to taker direction; own fills are not added to flow twice. Exact-money ledgers audit the complete accounting.

\section{Verified findings and their interpretation}
Across 35 training templates, \textbf{25,200 paired synthetic episodes} were audited; \textbf{83 tests} pass. One-hour runs start at $p_0=0.5$, $q_0=0$. Base fills assume 50 contracts ahead, 10\% participation and maker-fee multiplier 1. The table reports HJB net PnL and ES95, the average loss in the worst 5\% of episodes:

Fills remain assumed, all benchmark episodes extrapolate noise support, and settlement is synthetic. The new log-odds driver need not be a probability martingale: the preserved HJB is a reference policy, not a newly proven optimum or a historical profitability backtest.

\pagebanner{03 / REMAINING WORK}{Complete the Paper 2 Research Programme}
\takeaway{\textbf{Scope remaining.} Complete the paper's permanent-impact, spread, behavioral-profit, market-type and real-time toxicity analyses; then validate the integrated environment on held-out events.}

\subsection{1. Separate permanent and transitory impact}
Implement Glosten--Harris (Paper 2, Eq.~2) with the same response and market-level sample rules:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta L_{j,h}=b_{0,j}+b_{1,j}F_{j,h}
 +b_{2,j}(F_{j,h}-F_{j,h-1})+u_{j,h}.
 \label{eq:gh}
\end{equation}
$b_1$ and $b_2$ represent permanent and transitory impact. Define lags across quiet periods and compare nested fits with uncertainty. Kyle's slope alone does not establish permanent private-information impact.

\subsection{2. Measure effective spreads and settlement-based profitability}
Using observed as-of midpoint $M_i$ and actual outcome $Y_j$, reproduce effective half-spreads, passive-side profit and its decomposition (Paper 2, Eqs.~3, 6--7):
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{EHS}_i&=s_i(P_i-M_i),\qquad
 \Pi_i^{\rm maker}=(Y_j-P_i)m_i,\quad m_i=-s_ic_i, \label{eq:spreadprofit}\\
 \E[\pi^{\rm maker}]&=(w-\tfrac12)(A+B)+\tfrac12(A-B). \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{align}
Prices/payoffs are dollars; $w$ is the contract-weighted win rate and $A,B>0$ the mean win/loss per contract. Test YES-price miscalibration and frequency/magnitude edges. Audit quote staleness and distinguish the paper's before-fee aggregate profits from our net strategy PnL.

\subsection{3. Implement a trailing flow-toxicity signal}
Reproduce trailing VPIN (Paper 2, Eqs.~8--9) using complete \$500 dollar-volume buckets; split boundary-crossing trades proportionally. For YES/NO taker dollar volumes $V^B_\tau,V^S_\tau$:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{TVPIN}_t=\frac1{20}\sum_{\tau=t-20}^{t-1}
 \frac{|V^B_\tau-V^S_\tau|}{V_\tau},\qquad
 V_\tau=V^B_\tau+V^S_\tau.
 \label{eq:vpin}
\end{equation}
Predict next-bucket losses from prior buckets only. Apply the paper's 30--70 cent volume-weighted YES-price restriction and test alternative bucket sizes. VPIN is not an estimated fraction of informed traders.

\subsection{4. Reproduce the central market-type comparison}
Collect more \textbf{single-name} markets: the current sample has only one, versus 29 broad-based and 30 sports. Estimate the paper's cross-sectional model (its Eq.~4):
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{AS}_j=d_0+d_1\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{SN},j}+d_2^{\top}X_j+v_j.
 \label{eq:crosssection}
\end{equation}
$\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{SN},j}$ indicates single-name, $\mathrm{AS}_j$ is an impact/spread measure, and $X_j$ contains controls. Keep sports distinct and test settlement-conditioned evidence; report selection bias.

\subsection{5. Validate the integrated environment before policy claims}
Check full-simulator \emph{transaction-price} lambda; reference replay is already verified. Validate fills with ordered trades/book updates and queue/latency checks. Fit risk settings on train data, freeze them, and test held-out events with calendar cutoffs, dependence/coverage checks and dated fees. RL training/comparison remains subsequent capstone work.
